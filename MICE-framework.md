# MICE Quotient (MICE Framework)

*Developed by Orson Scott Card*

A story classification system based on what type of **question** drives the narrative. Most stories use multiple MICE elements nested within each other.

---

## The Four Story Types

### M - Milieu (Setting/World)

**Question:** "What is this place like?"

**Structure:** Story begins when character enters a new world/setting, ends when they leave

**Examples:**
- The Wizard of Oz
- Alice in Wonderland
- Gulliver's Travels
- Portal fantasies

**Focus:** Exploring and experiencing the environment

---

### I - Idea (Mystery/Information)

**Question:** "What's the answer?" or "How does this work?"

**Structure:** Story begins when a question is raised, ends when it's answered

**Examples:**
- Murder mysteries
- Scientific puzzles
- "Whodunits"
- Detective stories

**Focus:** Discovering information or solving a problem

---

### C - Character (Internal Change)

**Question:** "Who will this person become?" or "Can they change?"

**Structure:** Story begins when character becomes dissatisfied with their role, ends when they settle into a new role (or recommit to the old one)

**Examples:**
- Coming-of-age stories
- Redemption arcs
- Character studies
- Literary fiction

**Focus:** Internal transformation

---

### E - Event (External Threat)

**Question:** "Can they restore order?" or "How will this crisis be resolved?"

**Structure:** Story begins when the world falls out of balance, ends when balance is restored (or new order established)

**Examples:**
- War stories
- Disaster narratives
- Action/adventure
- Epic fantasies

**Focus:** Restoring equilibrium to the world

---

## Key Principles

### Nesting Elements

Most stories use **multiple MICE elements** nested within each other:

- **Open them in one order, close them in reverse order** (like nested parentheses)
- One element is usually **dominant** and determines the story's primary shape

**Example:** Star Wars (A New Hope)
- **Event** (dominant): Empire threatens galaxy → Rebellion wins
- **Character**: Luke is a farm boy → becomes a Jedi
- **Milieu**: Luke leaves Tatooine → explores the galaxy
- **Idea**: What are the Death Star plans? → Plans lead to its destruction

### Using MICE for Story Diagnosis

This framework helps with:

1. **Diagnosing story problems** - Did you open a question you never answered?
2. **Planning story structure** - Knowing when to start and end
3. **Understanding reader dissatisfaction** - Stories feel incomplete when MICE threads aren't properly closed
4. **Genre expectations** - Different genres emphasize different MICE elements

### Common Mistakes

- Opening a MICE thread and never closing it (leaving readers unsatisfied)
- Closing threads in the wrong order (creates structural awkwardness)
- Mixing incompatible dominant elements without clear hierarchy
- Starting the story too early or ending it too late for the dominant MICE type

---

## Practical Application

**Before writing:**
- Identify which MICE element is dominant
- Plan when to open and close each thread

**During revision:**
- Track each MICE thread through your story
- Verify all opened questions/threads are closed
- Check that they close in proper order (reverse of opening)

**If story feels off:**
- Map out your MICE threads
- Look for unclosed threads or mismatched opening/closing
